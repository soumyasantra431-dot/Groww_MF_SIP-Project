# Choosing a SIP problem: quantified evidence check and first-hand research guide

**7 November 2026 IST · Research instrument, not a selected segment or root cause.** Builds on the [person-level metric tree](PART2_METRIC_SYSTEM_AND_FIRST_RESEARCH_DECISION_2026-11-07.md), [original-source review](PART2_FOUR_LEAD_SOURCE_REVIEW_2026-11-07.md), and [decision-led plan](REMAINING_PROJECT_RESEARCH_PLAN.md). No participant was contacted, interviewed or observed while preparing this guide. It should be revised only when actual screening or a source correction shows a specific defect.

## 1. Purpose and decision

We need to choose a **first-time Groww mutual-fund investor segment**, a first-year moment and a problem that matters both to the investor and potentially to the 12-month active-SIP metric. The discovery engine suggests four leads; none yet measures the affected Groww cohort or proves a cause. This research asks:

1. **Existence:** does a target-fit investor actually experience an unwanted interruption or unmet need, rather than just ask a precautionary question?
2. **Mechanism:** what happened *before* their action, what did they want, what did they understand, and what did they do? Separate reported reason from technical cause.
3. **Outcome:** did a scheduled SIP instalment later complete, did another SIP keep the person active, or was a pause/stop deliberate and appropriate?
4. **Contrast:** what did a comparable continuer, recoverer or informed adjuster do under similar circumstances?
5. **Addressability and value:** which step can Groww influence, what existing control/workaround exists, and what user benefit would an intervention add?

**Decision rule:** use the answers to *compare problems*; choose the next research question if the mechanism or target fit remains open. A problem is not chosen because its current source count is largest or because it fits an AI demo. An informed pause is a valid user outcome, even when the North Star classifies the person inactive.

## 2. What can be quantified today, with arithmetic and limits

The bounded original-source review opened **six distinct public discussion roots**: the first-payment question, a large Coin payment thread, a Coin UPI thread, an AutoPay setup thread, a pause/delete thread, and a Coin-versus-AMC-SIP question. **Five of those six roots** contribute to at least one of the four intended leads; the Coin-versus-AMC-SIP root is a **misfit for trust/support** and belongs in control-choice context. The engine-listed Money Stack Exchange item was not re-opened, so it is not counted as reviewed here.

Lead assignments among the **five relevant reviewed roots** are: first-payment meaning **1**; payment/setup/recovery **3**; control meaning **2**, one of which is shared with first-payment meaning; trust/support **1**, shared with the payment thread but from a different writer. `1 + 3 + 2 + 1 = 7 lead-root assignments`, while the union is **5 distinct roots**. The difference of **2** reflects overlap, not two additional investors. **0/5** roots establishes a confirmed *first-ever MF + first SIP on Groww + first-year + next scheduled payment* case. This is **source-fit accounting**, not a 0% problem incidence. One adjacent Coin writer reported failure on two consecutive scheduled monthly debits; that is a reported subsequent due-date outcome, but it cannot be transferred to Groww's target cohort.

**What cannot be quantified publicly:** the eligible unique-person cohort `N`, month-12 active people `A`, the fraction affected by each mechanism, number of recoverable lost investors, user harm distribution, or incremental business value. The 932 retrieved store-review IDs and engine lead counts are not eligible-investor denominators. To estimate impact later, the minimum internal request is: unique matured target people by monthly payment state; verified failed-intended-payment events and recovery by next due date; informed pauses separately; other successful SIPs; verified actor/failure stage; and exposure to any proposed intervention. The scenario identity is `potential additional month-12 active people ≤ eligible month-12 inactive people with a Groww-addressable, unwanted pathway`. Both sides are presently unknown; do not assign a number or probability.

A first-payment interview must therefore separate the initial order, first scheduled due date, mandate state and final debit before interpreting a missing month.

## 3. Four equal opportunity cards: current evidence versus next discriminating observation

### A. First payment and next scheduled date

- **Reviewed support:** 1 independent forum root. A first-SIP writer reported cancelling the initial buy while uncertain about the next instalment.
- **Direct target fit and metric consequence:** 0 confirmed; later scheduled payment unknown.
- **Possible underlying need:** know the relationship between the initial order, recurring instruction and next due date. This is a **hypothesis**; a question alone does not prove harm.
- **Rival:** ordinary learning or an intended cancellation with no lost payment.
- **Distinguishing observation:** without showing a screen first, ask what the person expected immediately after the first payment, then what happened at the next due date. A safe task can test whether the current Groww journey makes that distinction clear.

### B. Setup failure, intended debit failure and payment-route workaround

- **Reviewed support:** 3 independent adjacent-platform roots. They represent **three different event stages**; one UPI follow-up belongs to the same root as its first report.
- **Direct target fit and metric consequence:** 0 confirmed; one adjacent account reports two consecutive intended debit failures, but later recovery and target-cohort outcome are unknown.
- **Possible underlying need:** a reliable route to complete a wanted contribution, or clear diagnosis when a route fails. Which actor caused a failure is unresolved.
- **Rivals:** mandate/bank/UPI condition, setup not yet complete, ordinary processing delay, or a correct platform state with misunderstood meaning.
- **Distinguishing observation:** first classify the event as mandate setup, scheduled due debit or separate payment; then ask what the person intended and what happened at the *following scheduled* instalment. If the bank/rail was the blocker, test Groww's ability to improve diagnosis or hand-off before claiming it can repair execution.

### C. Pause, delete, cancel and adjustment meaning

- **Reviewed support:** 2 roots (one shared with A). One writer described an accidental SIP deletion after a pause and asked about holdings and future investing; later action is unknown.
- **Direct target fit and metric consequence:** 0 confirmed; no verified subsequent lost instalment.
- **Possible underlying need:** confidently make a chosen change to future contributions while understanding what happens to existing holdings. **Helping an informed stop is also a valid outcome.**
- **Rival:** current controls may work as intended; the question may be reassurance without an adverse payment effect.
- **Distinguishing observation:** in the current Groww flow, ask what the investor intended each control to do, what they thought it would change, and what actually happened. Observe a non-transactional explanation task if the account points to a specific control.

### D. Trust or support concern after uncertainty

- **Reviewed support:** 1 relevant adjacent forum root. Its writer reported pausing investments through Coin amid concerns. A second engine-listed root about SIP modification is a **misclassification for this lead** and has been excluded here.
- **Direct target fit and metric consequence:** 0 confirmed; the reported pause is not verified as a stopped scheduled SIP.
- **Possible underlying need:** feel able to understand and resolve a consequential payment or control state without repeated outside effort.
- **Rival:** a deliberate risk-management decision, a concern about future redemption rather than current SIP payments, or circumstances outside the app.
- **Distinguishing observation:** reconstruct which event and support response came *before* the pause, what the next contribution was intended to do, and whether it occurred.

**Interim comparison:** B currently has the most reviewed *root variety* (**3**), while A, C and D each expose a different kind of question or reported consequence. B is therefore a useful **next research lane**, not automatically the highest-potential product opportunity. Every lead has **0 confirmed strict target cases** and an unknown eligible-population reach. A first-hand target-fit episode could reverse this order immediately. Do not add an arbitrary RICE score.

## 4. Screen people without recruiting only payment complaints

Recruit about **5–6** people for the brief's qualitative study. Use multiple routes if available, but keep source and response bias visible. The existing survey can identify possible follow-up volunteers **only where consent/contact permission allows**; its responses remain a separate secondary lane. Ask the Fellow to invite participants; the assistant does not contact them on its own. Do not ask participants to transact or share account numbers, PAN, balances, statements, bank messages or portfolio screenshots.

**Eligibility screen (ask before the interview, accept “not sure”):**

1. “Before the mutual-fund investment you are describing, had you ever invested in *any* mutual fund, on Groww or elsewhere?” Record first-ever MF **yes/no/unknown**. A past lump-sum MF purchase requires an explicit eligibility note; do not silently equate first SIP with first MF investment.
2. “Was your first mutual-fund SIP started through Groww?” Record **yes/no/unknown** and approximate start month/year. A first demat account is not a substitute.
3. “Has a year passed since that first SIP? If yes, approximately when?” Record **0–3, 4–6, 7–12, 13–18 months since start, or unknown**. The 13–18-month group can reconstruct the first year, but its recall is retrospective; people under 12 months cannot have an observed month-12 outcome yet.
4. “Would you be comfortable describing your experience without sharing account or financial identifiers?” Record willingness/consent and method (conversation or interactive written follow-up). Do not transfer respondent identifiers into the analysis file.

**Recruitment contrast:** aim to hear an uninterrupted continuer, a continuer who faced difficulty or recovered, an informed adjuster/pause, and someone with an unwanted interruption or unresolved status **if such eligible people are found**. These are *variation targets*, not quotas to manufacture from ineligible respondents. A “quiet drop-off” may be hard to reach; report that selection gap. Do not pre-screen only for complaints, or compare an experienced Coin investor with a first-time Groww investor as if matched.

**Invitation the Fellow may send, without the assistant contacting anyone:** “I’m studying people's first year of mutual-fund SIP investing for an academic project. Would you be willing to speak for about 20–30 minutes about what happened from your first SIP until now, including ordinary months? Stopping or changing is just as useful to understand as continuing. Please don't send account numbers, PAN, balances or screenshots. If you agree, I will use de-identified notes for the project; please also tell me whether you agree to AI-assisted analysis of those de-identified notes. You can skip questions or withdraw.” Record actual consent and date privately; do not treat silence as consent.

## 5. Neutral 20–30-minute interview guide

**Opening:** “I am studying what happens during people's first year of mutual-fund SIP investing. I am interested in ordinary and difficult months, including times when changing or stopping was the right choice. There is no correct answer. Please do not share account numbers, PAN, exact balances, bank messages or screenshots. You may skip any question.” Record consent, mode and approximate interview date without personal identifiers.

**Part A — purpose and expectation (open first, no lead names):**

1. “What were you hoping the first SIP would help you do? Why did you choose a recurring payment at that time?” Ask who or what influenced the decision **only after** their own reason.
2. “After you made the first payment, what did you expect to happen next, and when?” This tests schedule understanding without presupposing confusion.
3. “Walk me through your first SIP from starting it to now. What stayed routine? What changed, if anything?” Let them name a timeline before the interviewer names a problem.

**Part B — one actual month, routine or difficult:**

4. “Think of a recent month you remember clearly. What were you expecting your SIP to do that month?” If they have no difficult month, use an ordinary one. Ask whether they intended the scheduled payment, an edit, a skip, a pause or no action.
5. “What happened first, then what happened next? What did you see in Groww, your bank or elsewhere, if anything?” Ask for the *meaning* they took from any status and how sure they were; do not supply a cause menu.
6. “What did you do in the app and outside it? Did you contact anyone or use another tool? What happened after each step?” Distinguish advice received from action actually taken.
7. “What happened to the next scheduled instalment, as far as you know? How did you know? Was another SIP still running?” Accept **successful / unsuccessful / not due / deliberately stopped / unknown** as separate reported outcomes; do not call a debit alone final success.
8. “Did the result match what you wanted at the time? What effort, worry, missed intended action or benefit came from it?” This reveals user consequence without assuming continuation is always good.

**Part C — checking, alternatives and closing:**

9. “What was the last thing you checked about your SIP or fund, and what prompted you to check? What did you do with that information?” For cadence, ask about the last *two actual checks*; do not infer a weekly/monthly habit from a general answer.
10. “When you were unsure, what did you do outside Groww, if anything? What answer helped or failed to help?”
11. “If you wanted to keep contributing, change the amount/date, or stop for a while, what made that easy or difficult?” Ask this **after** their chronology so it does not steer the story.
12. “Is there anything I have misunderstood? Was a decision you made deliberately right for you even if it meant no payment?” Invite a counterexample and corrections.

**Probes only where the person reports the event:** distinguish mandate setup from due debit; pending from final success/reversal; one SIP from all SIPs; deliberate pause from failed intent; bank/rail/AMC signal from Groww's display; worry before an action from worry after it. Do not ask “Did market falls make you stop?” A market move may be context, not a root-cause label.

## 6. Coding and arithmetic after each real interview

Create a de-identified case ID, then record **stated / unknown / not applicable** for: first-ever MF; first SIP on Groww; approximate month since start; original job/goal; money-source or obligation change *only if stated*; intended next action; initiating event/stage; app signal; outside signal; participant interpretation; completed action; workaround; immediate payment status; **following scheduled payment status**; other active SIP; user consequence; existing control used; source of each statement; and the strongest rival. Distinguish `participant says`, `researcher observed`, `official documentation says`, and `PM inference`. Do not create a persona until multiple eligible episodes justify an actual situation profile.

At the study checkpoint report **counts first**, with denominators: `screened`, `eligible`, `interviewed`, `with a reconstructable episode`, `with stated intent`, `with a known next scheduled result`, `with an unwanted outcome`, `with an informed pause/change`, `with a comparable continuer`, and `unknown/unmatched`. For one **index event per person**, code the **reported next scheduled result** into one of four mutually exclusive states: `reported completed`, `reported not completed`, `no next instalment due because of a stated deliberate stop/completed tenure`, or `unknown`. Then `eligible interviewed = completed + not completed + no next due + unknown` if every eligible interview has an index event; otherwise add `no reconstructable index event` as a fifth disclosed bucket. Keep intent and the appropriateness of the choice on separate axes. These are self-reports, **not transaction-verified successes**. A person can have multiple events; retain non-index events separately. Do not divide `missed-payment stories / 5 interviews` and call it a Groww failure rate. The sample was selected for variation, not random estimation.

For each of the four leads, update: **number of independent public roots inspected; number of direct eligible interview cases; number with a known next-due result; number of counterexamples; type of observed consequence; actor plausibility; Groww's existing control; and what is still unknown**. Report a range only when its numerator and eligible denominator exist. If internal cohort telemetry becomes available, request reach and recovery rates with unique-person denominators and verify event coverage. If not, reach and NSM lift remain unknown; a rough industry SIP account figure must not fill them.

## 7. How the PM will choose, defer or reverse a problem

After the episodes, compare problems using the **same dimensions**, without a fabricated aggregate score: (a) directness and independence of evidence; (b) unwanted user consequence and its timing; (c) a plausible link to a qualifying month-12 payment; (d) current workaround and unmet need; (e) Groww-controllable step versus bank/rail/AMC dependency; (f) safe testability and the strongest rival; (g) any defensible reach proxy, otherwise unknown. A high complaint count in adjacent platforms does not override a direct, consequential Groww episode. The brief's delivery deadline can justify a reversible provisional MVP test, but it does not prove customer urgency.

**Select a provisional segment/problem only if:** at least one clearly eligible first-hand case exposes a concrete moment and consequence; a continuer, recovered case or informed adjustment challenges the proposed mechanism; the current Groww path has been checked; the proposed user value is separate from forced continuation; and there is a feasible disconfirming task test. More than one case improves confidence but an arbitrary interview count is not proof. If these are absent, explicitly retain a **research hypothesis** and narrow the MVP to testing a safe comprehension mechanism; do not claim the brief's validated target-segment interview requirement has been met.

**Reversal examples:** the issue is an informed pause; the relevant status was understood but an upstream debit failed; another SIP already makes the person active; the current control completes the task when found; or a comparable continuer has the same supposed “cause” without loss. In each case, rewrite or abandon the mechanism rather than rationalizing it. The first solution comparison is current Groww path **versus** simpler non-AI aid **versus** bounded AI aid for the *same task*. AI is chosen only if it helps people interpret variable circumstances more safely or usefully than the simpler option.
