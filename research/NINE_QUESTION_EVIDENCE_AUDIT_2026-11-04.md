# Nine-question evidence audit — original brief to next research decision

**Date:** 4 November 2026. **Purpose:** decide what existing research can honestly tell the discovery engine, what it cannot, and the smallest next evidence that could change a PM decision. This is a read of the original brief and dated project records, **not** a new source collection or fresh verification of every original post. Later engine specifications are not treated as research findings.

## Decision boundary and arithmetic

The brief's outcome is the percentage of eligible first-time mutual fund investors with at least one successful SIP instalment in the 30 days before their 12-month checkpoint. For a matured cohort, `N = A + I + U`: eligible investors with confirmed active, confirmed inactive, and unresolved outcomes. An exact rate needs the eligible-person denominator and reliable success events; neither exists in the public material. One investor with several SIPs still counts once. A reduction can still leave an investor active; a legitimate pause may be good for the investor while lowering this metric. The brief's examples of busy lives, underwhelming statements and lapse are prompts to investigate, not established causes.

**Read these records with their original limits:** [first problem map](PROBLEM_MAP_AND_FIRST_DECISION.md), [first research plan](MERGED_RESEARCH_PLAN.md), [official and pilot findings](FIRST_PASS_FINDINGS.md), [survey analysis](SURVEY_ANALYSIS_2026-10-29.md), [population and journey pass](population_journey_2026-10-30/findings.md), [behaviour-sequence pass](behaviour_sequences_2026-10-30/findings.md), and [explanation comparison](explanation_comparison_2026-10-30/findings.md). The original assignment is the user-supplied NextLeap brief. Source-attributed reports below verify that an account was recorded, not that its transaction or motive was independently verified.

## Question-by-question audit

### 1. Why do people start a SIP, and what do they expect?

- **Existing answer:** Industry survey material describes broad investor motives; selected public accounts mention a goal, salary-linked habit, parental or friend influence, or expectations about returns. One survey respondent combined a long-term purpose with an early “profits” expectation. These are different populations and a few self-reports, not the typical motive of first-time Groww SIP starters.
- **Missing:** The person's own starting purpose, expected first-year experience and timeline, money source, and whether their expectation later affected a contribution decision. The company positioning cannot fill these personal fields.
- **Smallest useful evidence:** Ask a consenting, self-described first-time Groww SIP respondent: “What were you trying to achieve when you began, what did you expect in the first months, and what happened?” Keep the expectation in their words before offering choices.
- **Decision changed if learned:** Distinguishes an expectation/meaning opportunity from a payment or affordability problem; no feature follows automatically.

### 2. What changes in behaviour in the months after starting?

- **Existing answer:** Official sources map activities people *can* perform; selected accounts describe checking, adjusting, catching up, holding or stopping. The behaviour-sequence pass correctly separates contemplated, attempted and completed action. It is not a longitudinal sample of the same eligible people.
- **Missing:** A within-person month-by-month sequence from the first completed instalment, including ordinary months, changed contributions and an observed ending. We cannot name a common drop-off month.
- **Smallest useful evidence:** One first-year timeline with approximate months, asking what changed after the first successful instalment and what happened at the next due date. Seek both a continuer and an interrupted account before generalising a pattern.
- **Decision changed if learned:** Locates a candidate moment, or shows that an apparent shift in app use did not change SIP activity.

### 3. What leads investors to pause, reduce or stop?

- **Existing answer:** Contrasting public reports describe intended payments facing execution trouble, uncertainty about status, changing money priorities, and concern about returns. Other reports show continuation despite concern. These explanations can overlap and many examples are outside the exact cohort. A smaller amount is not automatically inactive; a failed instalment does not prove the investor wanted to pay.
- **Missing:** In the same target-fit episode: intent before the due date, the actual system event, action taken, final contribution status, and whether stopping was wanted. No cause mix for Groww's cohort is measured.
- **Smallest useful evidence:** A first-hand account of a *specific* change or missed due date: “What did you want the next contribution to do? What happened, and what did you do next?” Ask for a recent ordinary due date as contrast.
- **Decision changed if learned:** Separates an unwanted interruption from a supported, informed pause; points to the appropriate process or user need.

### 4. What do investors check, and how often?

- **Existing answer:** Documentation shows returns, statements, payment status and support touchpoints; selected accounts report checking returns/status or using a spreadsheet. They establish examples of *what* and *why*, not a frequency distribution. App removal or fewer visits does not establish SIP lapse.
- **Missing:** A defined checking interval and purpose for target users, plus whether checking led to any contribution decision. Public posts seldom supply a reliable denominator or regular frequency.
- **Smallest useful evidence:** Ask a target-fit respondent about the **last two actual checks**: when, what they sought, what they saw, and what action followed. A task diary or app analytics would be needed for rates; do not estimate them from posts.
- **Decision changed if learned:** Shows whether checking is a relevant decision moment or only a monitoring habit; “how often” may remain unknown.

### 5. What confuses or worries them inside the app?

- **Existing answer:** Public accounts report uncertainty about debit-bank linkage, failed/pending states, holding-format effects and cancellation/restart meaning. Groww help pages document some controls, but documentation is capability evidence, not proof of comprehension or usability. Concern need not produce a failed payment or exit.
- **Missing:** Which screen/version a first-year Groww user saw, their interpretation, attempted action and actual task outcome. Some public accounts are precautionary questions, not experienced failures.
- **Smallest useful evidence:** Observe one consenting target user carrying out a safe, non-transactional explanation or navigation task, or ask them to recount a real recent screen with no portfolio screenshot or identifiers. Compare their interpretation with the documented state.
- **Decision changed if learned:** Distinguishes status comprehension or discoverability from an underlying payment execution failure.

### 6. What do they do outside the app when unsure?

- **Existing answer:** Selected accounts mention a bank, platform support, friends, a forum or a separate purchase. These are reported steps; a reply from another poster or an intention to contact support is not a completed workaround.
- **Missing:** Sequence, purpose and result of each outside-app step for a target-fit investor. We cannot tell whether an outside purchase completed the intended scheduled SIP.
- **Smallest useful evidence:** In the same concrete episode, ask: “What did you do elsewhere, what were you trying to settle, and how did you know the issue ended?” Record bank, person, community or other platform only as stated.
- **Decision changed if learned:** Reveals the actual workaround and where Groww could help without inventing platform fault.

### 7. What separates continuers from quiet drop-offs?

- **Existing answer:** The records include continuers, recovery attempts and people who changed one SIP but continued another. This is useful counterevidence to “one cancellation equals investor churn.” The seven survey responses contain ordinary-completion reports but no adequately reconstructed interrupted target case. Public conversation sampling cannot see people who simply go quiet.
- **Missing:** Comparable first-time Groww investors exposed to similar circumstances, with known subsequent contribution outcomes. No matched comparison, silent-drop-off denominator or causal protective factor is available.
- **Smallest useful evidence:** Pair a target-fit continuer's **difficult month** with an interrupted respondent's episode, asking the same neutral sequence and checking the next contribution outcome. Groww cohort data would be required to size any difference.
- **Decision changed if learned:** Helps distinguish adaptation/recovery from absence of friction; cannot by itself prove why one group retained.

### 8. How do patterns differ by income stability, age, money source or influence?

- **Existing answer:** Industry and company publications supply broad demographic or product context; a few public accounts state parent funding, salary, unemployment or friend influence. These sources use different populations and units. They do not establish a Groww first-year segment pattern.
- **Missing:** Stated circumstances and the same defined episode/outcome across enough comparable target-fit cases. Age, city or ticket size cannot be used to infer income stability, motive or affordability.
- **Smallest useful evidence:** Add only **voluntary broad bands or stated circumstances** to the same episode intake, then compare like-with-like cases. Leave fields unknown when unstated. Any reach estimate needs an eligible-user denominator from Groww.
- **Decision changed if learned:** Suggests a context for further study; it does not validate a demographic segment or opportunity size.

### 9. What unmet needs recur across conversations?

- **Existing answer:** The current accounts suggest *candidate* needs around knowing a payment's final state, understanding control effects, fitting contributions to changing circumstances and interpreting results. These are researcher interpretations across selected material, with rival explanations and existing controls. The source mix and target fit are too thin to claim a consistently recurring first-year Groww need.
- **Missing:** Independently attributed, target-relevant episodes across suitable source types, each with a stated consequence or workaround; evidence that existing options do not already meet the need. Multiple comments in one thread are not independent confirmations.
- **Smallest useful evidence:** Expand only a **permitted**, question-led corpus with both ordinary-success and difficult episodes; group duplicate/reposted accounts, then review a small contrast set by hand. Follow up one candidate need with a real task or episode before naming a customer problem.
- **Decision changed if learned:** Makes an opportunity comparison defensible while leaving reach, cause and solution impact explicitly unknown.

## Readiness decision

**Ready to define a bounded discovery-engine job:** source admission and cohort-fit labels; source-backed extraction of purpose → expectation → circumstance → intention → event → action → outcome; unknown and counterexample handling; deterministic counts using source roots/episodes and explicit denominators; nine evidence-bounded answers; and human review. The original plan already identifies these distinctions. The engine may surface candidate problems for a researcher to compare, but cannot measure Groww's NSM, observe quiet drop-off, infer population prevalence from public posts, or choose a feature.

**Before claiming a working research engine:** establish a source-by-source usable/permission basis, obtain a relevant and honestly counted user-voice corpus, freeze a small genuinely human-labelled evaluation set, and verify that outputs cite sources and preserve unknowns. A source family listed in the brief is an example of possible evidence, not proof that a live connector exists. Mock stories can test the interface, but cannot fill research gaps or increase evidence volume.

**Immediate PM move:** do the smallest combined intake/review that can expose a real first-year sequence and a continuer contrast; record coverage against all nine questions. If first-hand access remains limited, build with truthful unanswered states and disclose the gap. Do not delay the research instrument until the winning segment, cause or moment is known. Preserve source records and contrary cases; archive irrelevant plans from the active path rather than deleting the audit trail.
