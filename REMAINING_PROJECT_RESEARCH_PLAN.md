> **7 November progress update:** Part 2 metric arithmetic is documented. The current Part 4 **provisional** problem is an intended first-year Groww SIP payment that does not clearly complete; see the [dated problem decision](PART4_PROVISIONAL_PROBLEM_DEFINITION_2026-11-07.md). Earlier cash-flow-adjustment documents are retained as decision history and an informed-pause guardrail. No target-cohort interview, observed task, verified root cause, measured reach/lift, or deployed investor MVP is claimed. The plan below is the method; the dated decision records show how it changed with evidence.

**Scope:** Parts 2–8 and the final deliverables. This plan follows the brief's North Star Metric and the current discovery engine. It does not claim a chosen problem, completed interviews, validated AI accuracy, or a deployed investor MVP.

**Method:** Work backwards from an investor outcome and the brief's business outcome. For each proposed conclusion, ask: *What would I have to observe for this to be true? What else could produce the same observation? What would change my decision?* The sections below specify the work, rather than using a framework name as a substitute for reasoning.

## How we will reason from outcome to evidence to decision

### 1. Set the two outcomes before looking for a cause

The **investor outcome** is being able to make an informed, affordable choice about an intended SIP contribution and know what happened to it. Continuing unchanged is only one possible good result; a deliberate reduction, pause or stop may better serve the investor. The **business outcome** is the brief's unique-person month-12 active-SIP percentage. We must show a plausible chain between them: `investor circumstance or system condition → investor interpretation/action → payment or adjustment outcome → possible month-12 active state`. Each arrow is a separate claim. A source may support the first two arrows without supporting an effect at month 12.

The project deadline makes **delivery** urgent. It does not establish that a particular investor problem is widespread or newly urgent. For a chosen problem, test *user urgency* with the consequence in a real episode (missed intended contribution, time spent, uncertainty, support burden, or inability to make a wanted change), and *business importance* with the causal path and any defensible reach data. If reach or incremental business value has no denominator, say unknown. This prevents an attractive UI or large complaint pile from becoming the reason to choose a problem.

### 2. Keep a claim ledger, not a pile of quotes

For every material finding, record: claim; unit (person, payment, source root, rupees); eligible/adjacent/unknown cohort fit; original link or consented respondent ID; date; observed or reported event; what the source actually says; interpretation; rival explanation; missing ending; and decision it could change. Label **company documentation**, **public first-person account**, **survey answer**, **interview report**, **observed task**, **model proposal**, and **researcher inference** separately. A help page proves a control is documented, not that a first-time investor found or used it. Several replies in one forum thread are not several independent investors; deduplicate by source root and report question-specific denominators.

Audit each of the engine's nine questions as `supported / partial / conflicting / unanswered`. Attach a relevant source, contrasting account and missing field. Do not turn the Fellow-endorsed draft readings into approved source facts. Review direct Groww first-year accounts before promoting an adjacent-platform mechanism. Do not infer first-time status, month, intent, successful payment or income stability from an app name, age or phrase such as “new investor.” The survey is a separate, small secondary lane. Quantify **this reviewed corpus** where useful; never present its share of complaints as the share of Groww investors.

#### Use the discovery engine as an evidence index and hypothesis generator

At the start of each Part 2–4 decision, open the engine's nine-question view and the source links behind the relevant answer. Q1–Q2 locate starting goals and changing behaviour; Q3 and Q5–Q6 locate an interruption, interpretation, and workaround; Q4 tests whether checking is a real decision moment; Q7–Q8 supply continuer and circumstance contrasts; Q9 groups candidate unmet needs. Pull **episodes**, not just theme names: original goal, first-year timing, intended action, event, app/bank signal, reported reason, outside action, immediate outcome, next scheduled payment, and whether each field is stated or unknown. Review the original source for every claim that could influence segment or problem choice. Record the number of independent reviewed source roots, direct versus adjacent cases, contradictory cases, and missing endings beside the finding. The current nine public readings and four leads are starting queries, not nine verified answers or a ranked opportunity list.

For each candidate insight, write four lines in the decision log: **observation** (what an inspected account reports), **interpretation** (what it may mean for the user's progress), **rival** (another plausible explanation of the same account), and **decision implication** (what we would test or change). Depth means following the sequence and consequence in a few strong accounts, including ordinary success, rather than inflating the count of short mentions. If a question has only feature documentation or missing outcomes, the engine should produce a research question, not a confident user claim. A survey answer can corroborate or challenge a mechanism, but its separate sampling and consent limits stay visible.

#### Hypotheses are allowed, with a reliability rule

A hypothesis is a **testable explanation**, not a substitute for a missing fact. Write it as: `For [eligible investor and circumstance], when [event], [proposed mechanism] may lead to [observable action/outcome], because [inspected supporting accounts].` Add its strongest rival, what should be observed if it is true, what should be observed if the rival is true, and the smallest realistic test that separates them. Mark its basis as `single source`, `several independent roots in one family`, `several families`, or `direct target-cohort plus contrast`; these are descriptions of evidence breadth, **not probabilities or proof**. Record whether the accounts establish intent, payment finality, and the next scheduled outcome. A mechanism with an unknown ending is a valid hypothesis but not a validated continuation problem.

For example, **illustrative hypothesis, not a Groww finding**: an investor who intended to pay may see an ambiguous pending state, assume the scheduled SIP succeeded, and miss the chance to resolve a payment-route issue before the next due date. The rival is that the investor understood the status but the bank/mandate blocked payment regardless of Groww's explanation. A distinguishing interview asks what the investor believed at the time, what each system showed, what action they took, and whether the next payment completed; a safe task test checks whether clearer status changes understanding or action. If the investor already understood the state, or no wanted payment was lost, we narrow or reject the hypothesis. We would not infer the frequency of this sequence from public complaint volume.

Before a hypothesis becomes the chosen problem, require a target-fit episode or an explicit provisional choice, a concrete user consequence, a continuer or other counterexample, a credible Groww-controlled point of intervention, and a named observation that could falsify the mechanism. If those are absent, use the hypothesis to guide research; do not turn it into a slide headline stating a root cause. Even a well-supported mechanism may have unknown reach and unknown 12-month effect. The product decision must state those limits and why it is still proportionate to test.

### 3. Build a state model before looking for an intervention

Start at the month-12 checkpoint. Let `N` be eligible, matured **people**, `A` people with a qualifying successful instalment in the preceding 30 days, `I` people confirmed without one, and `U` people whose payment status is unresolved. Then `N = A + I + U`; the brief's percentage requires `A / N` once payment finality and the handling of `U` are specified. The public corpus supplies neither `N` nor `A`, so no baseline or lift is calculated. As a **hypothetical arithmetic illustration only**, if `N = 1,000`, `A = 700`, `I = 200`, and `U = 100`, the confirmed-success lower bound is `700 / 1,000 = 70%` and the possible upper bound is `(700 + 100) / 1,000 = 80%`. The exact rate remains unresolved until the 100 unknown statuses are settled. These are not Groww data.

For each person's first year, map scheduled due dates, attempted debits, final successful instalments, reversals, deliberate controls, recovery and platform changes. Use a **state transition** rather than a simple funnel: an investor can fail one payment and recover, reduce one SIP and remain active through another, or deliberately pause and later restart. Keep three axes separate: (a) what payment status is observed, (b) what the investor intended, and (c) what reason or actor is reported or verified. A bank/mandate failure, a Groww status misunderstanding and a chosen pause may all produce “no successful instalment” but demand different responses. Never add overlapping reason counts and call the total unique people.

At each candidate moment, ask two questions. **Arithmetic:** which transition would have to change for this work to affect the month-12 numerator, and could another SIP or later recovery already make the person active? **Welfare:** would changing it help the investor accomplish an intended, suitable action? This rules out a feature that merely makes pauses harder. A scenario calculation, if needed, is `additional active people = eligible people exposed × incremental active-state difference`; both inputs need a defensible source, and the result is a scenario, not a forecast. Check whether cost, unnecessary contributions, or shifted support work offset the benefit. Request Groww event data only for rates the public cannot supply: eligible cohort size, monthly person-level active states, payment finality and reversals, reason/actor categories, recovery by next due date, and adjustments before stopping. Until available, mark reach and effect size unknown.

### 4. Compare the four leads using the same questions

Create one evidence card per lead. For each, write: the situation and eligible population; stated user goal; initiating event; observed action and **next scheduled payment**; consequence; workaround; current Groww control; evidence directness/independence; alternative explanation; potential month-12 pathway; who can act (Groww, bank, mandate rail, AMC, investor); user benefit; and missing test. Compare **like units and horizons**. For example, a first-payment interpretation account cannot be compared with a month-9 cancellation post as if both measured the same month-12 loss.

Use four tests of an opportunity, without an invented weighted score: **real** (is the unwanted event or need demonstrated in target-fit episodes?); **important** (what concrete consequence, severity and recurrence are evidenced, and what is unknown about reach?); **addressable** (can Groww change the relevant step, given bank/rail/AMC roles and existing controls?); **worth choosing now** (could a safe intervention be tested before the deadline, with a credible route to user and business value?). A severe single case may warrant attention but cannot supply prevalence. The easiest research question is not automatically the best product opportunity. If the relative order flips when unknown reach or actor responsibility changes, state that the comparison is unstable and choose the next discriminating research action, not a winner.

Preserve at least these rivals: upstream bank/mandate failure; ordinary learning that resolves without harm; returns anxiety without contribution change; a wanted affordability-driven pause; and an existing control that works but was not used or understood. Check the real Groww journey before calling a gap “missing feature”; separate availability, findability, comprehension and reliability. Competitor products may show a current alternative or feasibility, but “competitor has it” does not prove user value or a Groww gap.

### 5. Segment by situation and behaviour first; check demographics as modifiers

The target cohort is fixed by the brief. Within it, form **candidate research segments**, not presumed market clusters, from combinations that can change the intervention: `investor's intended next contribution × circumstance × observed payment/control outcome × interpretation and workaround`. Consider, for example, an investor who intended to pay but could not tell whether the instalment completed; one who deliberately adjusted an unaffordable amount; and one who encountered the same uncertainty yet recovered. These are *candidate situations*, not findings or a final segment.

First describe the successful paths, not just failure: a stable payer with no reported difficulty; someone who recovered from a failed attempt; someone who adjusted an amount/date and remained active; and someone who chose an informed pause. These categories are analytically distinct and must not be assigned population shares from posts. Compare continuers and interrupters **within a comparable difficult month**, rather than comparing all happy users with all complainants. Record duration since first SIP, mandate/payment route, whether one or several SIPs existed, obligation or income change **only if stated**, and whether the next payment completed. This can reveal a protective process (clear status, buffer, workable adjustment, trusted automation) or disconfirm it. It cannot establish a causal protective factor from 5–6 selected people. Age, city and income bands may matter, but do not substitute them for actual money source, intent or ability to act. Ask whether a proposed segment can be recognised in the product without sensitive inference and whether its need differs from the nearest alternative segment.

Segment choice requires: a target-fit episode; a similar contrasting episode or explicit account of why the contrast is absent; a distinct need and moment; a plausible Groww-controlled response; and a user-valued outcome. Size and probability of success remain unknown if there is no Groww cohort denominator. If no candidate meets this bar, make a **provisional, reversible** choice for an MVP test and label the weaker links. Never claim that the few people we recruited represent the full segment.

### 6. Run primary research as a test of competing mechanisms

Use **semi-structured critical-incident interviews**: a consistent set of neutral prompts about one actual event, with follow-up questions determined by what the person says. This method fits the missing evidence: intent, sequence, meaning, workaround and the next scheduled outcome. A broad survey would collect more opinions but rarely reconstruct a payment episode; analytics would locate failure rates but not explain intent; a usability test would reveal an interface problem but cannot alone show that it caused a real interruption. Complementary methods answer different parts of the claim.

Screen first-ever mutual-fund investment, first SIP on Groww, time since first start, relevant recent experience, and willingness to discuss a de-identified event. Recruit through the existing survey **only if its consent and contact route allow follow-up**, then other direct voluntary channels if needed. Aim for 5–6 eligible people and contrasting states, including ordinary continuers; record screening exclusions and who is missing. Do not silently redefine the target cohort because direct cases are hard to recruit. If only written one-shot answers arrive, report written responses, not completed interviews. A back-and-forth written interview with actual follow-up probes should be described as such.

Interview order: (1) original goal, who influenced the choice, expected timeline and affordability; (2) an open first-SIP-to-now chronology, including ordinary months, changes and the last scheduled payment, **before naming a problem or asking for a difficult month**; (3) a normal month, what they checked and why; (4) one **specific** difficult or changing month if the person reports one, without suggesting a cause; (5) what was due, what the app/bank showed, what they thought, what they did inside and outside Groww, and the immediate and next scheduled outcomes; (6) how they would have recognised a satisfactory resolution; (7) only after the story, ask about existing controls and alternative help. For continuers, ask the same event sequence **only when the event type is actually comparable**; predefine broad event types before recruitment and keep an unmatched/other lane. Probe “What makes you say that?” and “Could anything else explain it?” without arguing. Record `event reported`, `participant interpretation`, `researcher hypothesis`, and `unverified payment finality` as different fields. Self-reported timing can suffer hindsight bias; mark uncertainty. Do not ask them to show messages, balances, account identifiers or portfolio screenshots.

After each interview, write an episode record before synthesising. Compare cases on the same fields, then look specifically for a case that contradicts the leading explanation. Triangulate the public-engine lead, interview report and any official capability claim **without averaging them**. If the account lacks the next-payment ending, ask a follow-up if consent allows; otherwise keep it unknown. Five or six sessions can make a mechanism credible and a design testable, but cannot estimate its prevalence or prove that a UI change raises the North Star.

### 7. Define a problem with a causal boundary, then test solutions

Use a problem statement that can be falsified: `For [eligible investor in stated circumstance], when [specific first-year event], [observed obstacle] prevents [wanted progress], leading to [supported consequence]; the current workaround is [observed]. We believe Groww can influence [specific step], while [strongest rival] remains possible.` Distinguish **symptom** (e.g., unclear payment status), **reported reason**, **supported mechanism** and **ultimate technical cause**. If only the symptom is evidenced, frame the problem at that level. “Markets fell” remains context, not a complete mechanism. Choose the problem only after the four-lead and continuer contrasts; record why the next strongest alternative lost **for now** and what finding would reverse the choice.

Then compare at least three mechanisms for the *same user task*: current Groww flow/workaround; a simpler non-AI explanation, status view or operating fix; and an AI aid. Consider whether the source of confusion is missing information, difficult navigation, uncertain payment finality, or an actual failed debit. An LLM cannot repair an upstream failed debit or verify account-specific success without authoritative status data. Its possible role is bounded interpretation of messy user language against official, current guidance, with an explicit unknown/fallback. Select the smallest safe intervention whose incremental value can be tested. Write the predicted user behaviour and product outcome **before** building, plus a disconfirming result. Do not keep an AI idea merely because the brief requires an AI-native deliverable or a prototype already exists.

### 8. Validate the MVP at three distinct levels

**Output validity:** freeze a representative human-labelled golden set for the chosen MVP task, including ambiguity, negation, changed amount, bank-versus-app attribution, legitimate pause, no account status, and unsafe/advice requests. Measure supported correct fields, missed fields, invented facts, unsafe claims, source fidelity and appropriate abstentions. A model agreeing with itself is not independent validation. The discovery engine's extraction test is a different task and cannot automatically be reused as the MVP quality score. Any severe false payment assurance or investment advice blocks release of that answer path; show an official-source or “cannot determine” fallback instead.

**Task value:** with at least three target users, compare what they can do with the current/ordinary guidance and the MVP on equivalent real, de-identified uncertainties. Record starting information, task, assistance, correct understanding, appropriate next action, time/effort if meaningful, trust, harmful misread and fallback usefulness. Counterbalance task order where feasible; do not coach one version more than the other. A functional page or a pleasing answer is not task success.

**Business effect:** the short test cannot measure 12-month continuation. Define a future Groww instrumentation/experiment plan: eligible-person cohort, exposure unit, assignment or suitable comparison, payment finality, month-12 outcome, guardrails, and how to handle users whose pause is appropriate. A leading indicator such as correct status comprehension is a **hypothesis** about future continuation until validated against payment outcomes. Do not present prototype-use rate, short-term clicks or model accuracy as North Star lift.

### 9. Make the final case study a traceable argument

The ten slides should follow the *decision chronology*, not merely display finished screens: brief metric and welfare tension → state model and unknown baseline → discovery-engine method and limits → source-backed candidate mechanisms → comparison and rival → first-hand test and what changed → segment and falsifiable problem → current/non-AI/AI solution comparison → MVP quality and user-task results → success, risks and reversal. On each material slide, use a source or participant ID, the unit/denominator for any number, and one sentence on what remains unproven. Do not import a Top Fellow's findings or visual density; borrow only the discipline of an evidence-to-decision chain and a clear evaluator walkthrough.

The earlier Myntra submission is a caution about **metric-unit mismatch, incomplete depth, and a discovery-engine link being submitted where the customer MVP link belonged**; its authenticated score did not explain *why* evaluators assigned each mark. For Groww, audit the final PDF, Evidence Log, engine URL and **separate MVP URL** against one frozen claim ledger. A reader should be able to trace `claim → original evidence → interpretation → alternative → product decision → test` in two minutes. Visual clarity does not compensate for an unsupported causal claim.

### Decision log and stopping rules for this project

For each material choice, write a dated line: **decision; user/business outcome; evidence IDs and denominators; strongest rival; what remains unknown; chosen next action; reversal condition**. If an interview contradicts a public lead, update the lead. If the only evidence is adjacent, narrow the claim. If no Groww event data exists, leave reach and month-12 lift unknown. If an existing control solves the task when found, test discoverability/comprehension before inventing a replacement. If the bank owns the failure, test whether Groww can improve diagnosis or recovery without claiming to fix the debit. If a pause is informed and beneficial, do not count preventing it as user value.

Stop collecting more of the **same** posts when they do not resolve cohort fit, intent, next-payment result or actor responsibility. Switch to a direct episode, observed task or internal-data request. Under the deadline, a reversible MVP test may proceed with an explicitly provisional problem; the deck must state the uncertainty rather than manufacturing confidence. The next immediate output is a *worked* metric-state tree and four evidence cards, followed by the neutral interview screen/guide. The feature decision comes after those comparisons and episodes.

## Part 2 — Decompose the business metric

**Decision served:** which changeable moment could plausibly increase the share of eligible investors with a successful SIP instalment at month 12, without pressuring an unsuitable continuation?

1. Freeze a measurement contract for a hypothetical Groww cohort: first-ever mutual-fund investor, first SIP start on Groww, day-zero event, month-12 checkpoint, and payment-finality rules. Ask a Groww data owner to resolve start event, reversal, original-versus-any SIP, and exclusion rules if internal access becomes available.
2. Arithmetic: `month-12 active rate = unique eligible cohort investors with ≥1 successful qualifying SIP instalment in the preceding 30 days / unique eligible cohort investors reaching the month-12 checkpoint`. Count one person once. Multiple SIPs, retries and rupees are separate units. Baseline and cohort size are **unknown** publicly.
3. Map the first-year journey: setup and mandate → first payment → repeated scheduled payments → checking and interpretation → deliberate adjustment or interruption → recovery or continued non-payment → month-12 state. Recovery can loop; do not multiply stage rates as if the funnel were strictly linear.
4. At each checkpoint, use mutually exclusive **observed payment states**: qualifying success, no qualifying success, or status unknown. Separately code intent and reason: intended payment blocked, informed pause/change, passive drift, moved platform/plan ended, or unknown. Do not force a bank failure, a deliberate pause and an unclear app status into one “churn” cause.
5. Build a metric tree with proximate product outcomes: correct setup, clear due/payment status, successful intended instalment, informed control use, recovery by the next scheduled payment, and appropriate continuation. Add guardrails for unwanted/pressured continuation and misleading advice.
6. For each node, attach the evidence and its limit. Public accounts can reveal a mechanism; only Groww cohort events could estimate node rates or the North Star. Internal data request, if available: month-1 through month-12 unique-person survival by cohort, payment outcome/reason/actor, skipped or edited before cancellation, recovery by next due date, direct versus regular route, and segment cuts. Keep the denominator and missing statuses in every cut.

**Output:** one metric tree, a one-page data dictionary and an unknowns list. **Gate:** every proposed opportunity has a plausible path to a qualifying month-12 payment and a separate user-value test; no public-post count is called reach.

## Parts 2–3 — Compare leads, then choose a provisional research segment

The discovery engine's four leads are: initial payment and next-date meaning; an intended payment that does not complete; consequences of pause/delete/cancel; and weakened trust after unclear payment or support. Compare them **without an automatic score** on source strength, observed user consequence, proximity to the metric, Groww's ability to influence it, rival explanation, and a feasible discriminating test. Mark reach and month-12 impact unknown.

The first **provisional** fieldwork focus is a first-time Groww mutual-fund investor in months 1–12 who intended to make a scheduled SIP payment but saw a failed, delayed, or unclear status. This is a recruitment hypothesis, **not** a selected root cause. It is a useful first test because an intended payment has a direct path to the qualifying event and the rival (bank/mandate condition versus app comprehension/recovery) is observable. Recruit comparable continuers who faced a difficult or confusing month so that a problem report is not mistaken for a drop-off cause. If direct Groww cases cannot be found, leave the opportunity unresolved and report the gap; do not promote Coin accounts into the target cohort.

## Part 3 — First-hand research methodology

**Purpose:** find the sequence and next scheduled-payment outcome that public accounts usually omit, and learn whether the investor wanted to continue, adjust, or pause.

- Recruit **5–6 eligible people**: approximately 3–4 with an intended payment interruption or uncertainty and 2 with a comparable difficult month who continued or made an informed change. Screen separately for first-ever mutual-fund investing, first SIP on Groww, and first-year timing; record unknown or ineligible honestly. The nine existing survey responses are a secondary lead and possible recruitment pool, not six interviews.
- Use a 20–30 minute, semi-structured **critical-incident interview** or interactive written exchange with follow-up probes if a call is impractical. A one-off form answer is a survey response, not an interview. The Fellow contacts participants and records consent; the assistant prepares questions and analyses de-identified notes. Do not request PAN, bank/account numbers, exact balances, portfolio screenshots or names in the research dataset.
- Start with the participant's goal and expected SIP behavior. Ask for one recent difficult or ordinary month: what was due, what they saw in Groww and outside it, what they believed, what they did, who helped, what happened immediately, and what happened at the **next scheduled instalment**. Ask what they normally check and when; probe a deliberate pause/change as a valid outcome. Avoid asking “Did market falls make you stop?” or suggesting a feature.
- Record each account as `intent → trigger/event → app/bank signal → interpretation → action/workaround → immediate result → next scheduled result`. Keep verbatim meaning, observed fact, participant belief and researcher inference separate. Make a comparable continuer/interrupter matrix; look for disconfirming cases.
- Stop a provisional explanation if a participant's account contradicts it; update the comparison. Five or six interviews can establish mechanisms and language, not population prevalence.

**Output:** recruitment and consent ledger without identifiers, neutral guide, 5–6 de-identified episode summaries if actually obtained, and a synthesis showing where primary accounts agree with or challenge the engine. If fewer are obtained, report actual count and use no simulated person as a participant.

## Part 4 — Define one problem only after the comparison

Write one problem statement with: **eligible segment; specific month-1 to month-12 moment; desired user progress; observed interruption; supported root-cause mechanism; existing workaround; product outcome; link to the North Star; strongest rival; unknown reach; reversal condition**. Show the chain `business metric → product outcome → discovery lead → first-hand episode → updated problem`. A market move can be context or trigger, never the entire root cause. If the mechanism is mainly an upstream bank issue or an informed pause, choose a different product problem or narrow Groww's role.

## Part 5 — Choose, build and evaluate an AI-native MVP

Compare the current Groww path, a simpler non-AI aid and one AI concept against the chosen task. AI earns its place only if it safely interprets messy, user-stated circumstances or ambiguous status in a way a fixed FAQ/control explanation misses. Do not add AI merely because the brief asks for it.

Build one narrow, real-task flow: input with private-data guard → bounded interpretation linked to an official product/payment-status source → clear options and uncertainty → safe fallback or decline when evidence is insufficient. Do not recommend funds, predict returns, assert a payment succeeded without confirmation, or nudge through fear or urgency. Deploy a public link that another person can operate. Measure its output on a **frozen, human-labelled task set** before enabling any high-stakes answer. The discovery-extraction golden set and MVP-task set may require different labels; put both in the same Evidence Log but never merge their agreement rates. If the same approximately 20 real de-identified items genuinely support both rubrics, document how.

## Part 6 — Test the MVP with users

Return to at least **three people from the chosen segment**. Let each bring a real, de-identified uncertainty or task; do not ask them to transact. Observe task completion, what they understood, whether they found and trusted the source, any mistaken inference, and whether the fallback was useful. Record actual task, result, quote/paraphrase, error, and change for the next iteration. A demo on fabricated scenarios is an engineering check, not a three-user test.

## Part 7 — Define success

- **Business:** the brief's unique-person month-12 active-SIP rate; baseline, sample and causal lift remain unknown without Groww data.
- **Leading outcomes:** for the selected eligible task, correct payment-status comprehension; appropriate next action; recovery of an **intended** missed payment by the next scheduled date; successful informed adjustment when continuing unchanged is unsuitable. Define each numerator, denominator, observation window and exclusions after the problem is chosen. Do not use clicks or answer length as success.
- **AI quality:** supported factual-field recall, unknown/abstention preservation, invented or unsafe claims, source-link correctness and task-level usefulness. Report `correct / wrong / abstained / unrun` with counts before percentages and a frozen golden-set denominator.
- **Guardrails:** false assurance of payment success, advice/recommendation output, pressured continuation, privacy leakage, unsupported actor blame, and repeat support contact caused by confusion. Any severe advice or payment-status error is a release blocker.

## Part 8 — Risks and mitigations

After choosing the actual MVP, rank only the risks that fit it. Likely candidates to test: wrong status interpretation, wrong attribution to Groww instead of bank/mandate rails, encouraging an unaffordable SIP, hiding a legitimate pause, weak source coverage, private financial details in input, poor fallback, and an apparent short-term task gain with no month-12 effect. Pair each with a detection signal, safe fallback, owner and reversal/rollback condition. Do not present a generic risk checklist as validation.
