## What the case study does and does not establish

The selected situation is a **reversible PM hypothesis**, the automation-reliant routine restorer after a previously working SIP payment becomes an unwanted repair task. The observed public burden supports testing an understanding-and-handoff response. It does not identify the failing actor, establish first-time cohort reach or prove month-12 retention lift. Existing Groww help and a fixed guide are explicit rival solutions.

The North Star is counted at the **eligible person** level: at least one finally successful SIP instalment in the 30 days before the first-SIP month-12 checkpoint. Several SIPs, retries or review posts do not create extra people. A responsible pause can be a good investor outcome even when it reduces short-term activity.

## Open brief requirements

No completed set of 5–6 target interviews, 3 real target-user MVP tasks, or independent ~20-item hand-labelled AI golden set is evidenced. The 9 survey submissions are secondary written responses, not substitutes. The fixed map is publicly testable and the live AI code is safe-fallback gated, but this is **not** claimed as a fully validated AI-native feature. These gaps are visible in slides 6 and 9 and in the Evidence Log.
