## Consequential unknowns and observations that could change the comparison

1. **Payment interruption versus normal processing.** A Groww first-time MF/SIP account with stated intended scheduled debit, actual bank/order/unit states, actions and **next scheduled payment outcome** would strengthen candidate 1. A separate one-time purchase, expected settlement lag, bank/mandate fault or normal next debit would weaken a Groww-specific comprehension/continuation claim. Source: original first-person account plus current Groww help as a capability/rival check. Neither verifies bank ledgers or cohort-wide reach.
2. **Difficult-month continuer.** A target-fit continuer who reports the same kind of constraint and a successful next scheduled payment could identify what differed. Without it, no matched contrast or protective-factor causal claim. Source: original first-person episode, not a generic “SIP is good” comment. This cannot prove why their outcome differed.
3. **Expectation/checking and affordability/control rivals.** Look for a stated decision link: what was checked/expected or what changed in finances/control understanding, what action followed, and what happened to the next due instalment. Mere checking, concern, holding units, a question or a documented feature does not support a continuation effect. Original accounts can show episodes; they cannot estimate prevalence or quiet drift.
4. **Input permission and variation.** About 20 varied, original, de-identified items are useful only if their source's terms support local text reuse and the proposed model-input step. Screen the source policy *before* copying text. If fewer than 20 qualify, freeze the actual number and leave evaluation unrun. Company help and the nine survey responses cannot pad the user-voice web input set.
