# Four discovery-engine leads: original-source review and PM decision

**7 November 2026 IST · Research artifact 2 · No segment or solution selected.**

## Why this review exists

**Decision:** which of the discovery engine's four proposed situations deserves a discriminating first-hand question, and what corrections must precede an opportunity comparison? **Method:** open the original public account behind each consequential lead; separate the initiating event, investor intent, action, reported outcome, actor and missing ending; compare with a rival. A forum author can report an experience but cannot independently verify the bank/AMC transaction or represent first-time Groww investors. No usernames, support-ticket IDs, financial identifiers or original post text are retained here. Store reviews, the survey and company documentation are not added to the user-account denominator. Read-only original-page review was done in Chrome; the Money Stack Exchange page did not load in this pass, so it remains unchecked.

The engine's [published comparison](https://sip-research-viewer.soumyasantra431.workers.dev/) contained **four assistant-proposed leads**, with **zero confirmed target-cohort cases** and **zero researcher-accepted source records** before this review. The root counts below describe *source roots reviewed in this pass*, not Groww people, prevalence or month-12 loss. A source may inform more than one question; do not sum card counts into unique people. This is an assistant original-source check, **not independent Fellow acceptance** or a validated model prediction.

## Card 1 — First payment versus next scheduled date

**Original:** [TradingQ&A first-SIP question, initial post and reply](https://tradingqna.com/t/sip-cancel-on-mf/190338/1). **Evidence class:** public first-person account plus a forum reply; **cohort fit:** first SIP is stated, but first-ever MF investment, Groww platform and first-year Groww status are not established. The reply describes Coin; treat this as an adjacent or eligibility-unknown mechanism, not a direct Groww case. **Independent roots inspected:** 1.

**What is reported:** the writer made an initial payment for a first SIP, was unsure when the next instalment would occur, cancelled the initial buy order, and asked about a refund. The reply explains that SIP frequency selected at creation governs later scheduling and describes a refund for the cancelled initial order. **Not established:** whether the recurring instruction remained, whether a later instalment was due or successful, whether the writer wanted to stop investing, and whether the explanation resolved the concern. The reply is guidance, not evidence of the writer's subsequent action.

**PM interpretation:** there is a real *reported question* about the relation between an initial buy and subsequent schedule. It is not yet evidence of an unwanted continuation failure. **Rival:** the investor may have made an informed cancellation after ordinary first-time learning, with no lost future payment. **Question that changes the decision:** “After you first paid, what did you expect to happen next, and what happened at the next scheduled date?” If the next payment worked or the stop was wanted, this lead narrows to education/control comprehension rather than an NSM problem.

## Card 2 — Intended payment, mandate setup and recovery are different stages

**Originals:** [Coin mandate-funded AMC SIP debit report](https://tradingqna.com/t/discontinuation-of-pooling-for-mutual-funds-and-how-it-affects-coin/120947/911); [Coin UPI payment question](https://tradingqna.com/t/peculiar-payment-problem-in-coin/156840/1) with its [same-writer follow-up](https://tradingqna.com/t/peculiar-payment-problem-in-coin/156840/9); [Coin AutoPay mandate-approval report](https://tradingqna.com/t/introducing-upi-autopay-for-sip-payments/173064/3). **Evidence class:** public first-person accounts; **cohort fit:** Coin/adjacent, not confirmed first-time MF or Groww first year. **Independent roots inspected:** 3; the original UPI post and follow-up are one dependent account.

**What is reported, separated by stage:**

1. The AMC SIP writer reports that an expected mandate-funded debit failed to occur in two successive months and says they could not manually pay the existing order. This is a reported **scheduled-payment execution interruption**. Their stated attribution to a backend problem is a user report, not a technically verified root cause. The next successful instalment after the second report is unknown.
2. The UPI writer first reports being unable to pay through the intended bank/UPI route. Months later, the same writer says they changed UPI apps and current payments work. This is a **reported payment-route workaround**, but the post does not establish a successful *next scheduled SIP instalment* or first-year continuity.
3. The AutoPay writer reports a **mandate approval error during setup**. No scheduled SIP debit or later instalment outcome is stated. Coding this as an interrupted scheduled instalment would be wrong.

**PM interpretation:** the original engine lead combined setup, due-payment execution and workaround into one broad “payment recovery” theme. It is a useful *investigation lane* but not a single cause, exit form or recovered-person count. **Rivals:** bank/UPI/mandate condition, processing delay, platform-side routing, or a manual-route constraint; different items may have different causes. **Question that changes the decision:** in a direct Groww first-year account, establish the **event stage** first: “Was a mandate being created, was a scheduled payment due, or were you trying a separate payment? What result did you see, and what happened at the following due date?” Only then ask about app, bank or outside actions. This is a high-information research question, **not** a claim that payment failures are the largest opportunity.

## Card 3 — Pause/delete/cancel meaning

**Original:** [Coin SIP pause-then-delete question and reply](https://tradingqna.com/t/delete-option-in-sip-conditional-order-in-coin/96985/1). The [first-payment account in Card 1](https://tradingqna.com/t/sip-cancel-on-mf/190338/1) also informs cancellation meaning but must not become a second independent source. A third engine-listed [Money Stack Exchange account](https://money.stackexchange.com/questions/102035/icici-redeem-mutual-fund-sips-through-net-banking) was **not reverified in this pass**. **Evidence class:** public first-person question plus forum replies; **cohort fit:** Coin/adjacent or unknown; **distinct original roots inspected here:** 1 primary control root plus the shared Card 1 root.

**What is reported:** the writer says they paused one SIP, then accidentally deleted it. They ask whether deletion redeems existing holdings and whether they can invest again. A reply says holdings are not redeemed and another SIP can be created; the writer asks for clarification. **Not established:** whether any later SIP was created, any future instalment was missed, or the investor's overall SIP activity changed. The reply addresses a product distinction; it does not independently verify the user's account outcome or current Groww behaviour.

**PM interpretation:** distinguish the *SIP instruction*, *past invested holdings* and *future contribution*. The reported confusion is real in this adjacent account; a retention impact is not shown. **Rival:** the control may work correctly and the person may be seeking reassurance after an otherwise reversible choice. **Question that changes the decision:** for a Groww first-year investor, ask what they intended a pause, skip, edit, delete or stop to change, what they expected would happen to current holdings and the next due instalment, and what actually happened. A safe current-app task can test comprehension without a transaction.

## Card 4 — Trust/support after an unclear process: one valid lead, one misfit

**Originals:** [Coin trust/pause account](https://tradingqna.com/t/discontinuation-of-pooling-for-mutual-funds-and-how-it-affects-coin/120947/935) and [Coin SIP versus AMC SIP question](https://tradingqna.com/t/coin-sip-vs-amc-sip/127618/1). **Evidence class:** public first-person reports; **cohort fit:** adjacent Coin/Zerodha; **independent roots inspected:** 2, but only **one** belongs under the proposed trust/pause lead.

**What is reported:** in the first account, a writer expresses concern about support and future redemption and says they have paused investments through Coin. This is a **reported choice and concern**, not a verified stopped scheduled SIP or a first-year Groww episode. Their next payment and platform-migration outcome are unknown. The second account says the writer is new to Zerodha and asks about the difference between Coin and AMC SIP because they want modification control and cannot find matching support guidance. It **does not report trust loss, a payment interruption or a pause**.

**Correction to engine proposal:** remove the second source from the trust/support evidentiary basis; it belongs, at most, in a **control-choice/findability context** lane. Do not count a question about modification as a trust-related stop. This is a correction of an assistant-proposed research grouping, **not a correction of a live model prediction**. **Rival for the remaining trust lead:** the pause may reflect a deliberate risk-management choice or concerns beyond the support experience, and it may not concern an SIP at all. **Question that changes the decision:** “What happened before you decided to pause, what did you want the next contribution to do, and did that payment occur or was it intentionally stopped?”

## Cross-card synthesis: facts, hypotheses, and next move

**Source-attributed facts within this bounded review:** one writer asked about first versus next payment after cancelling an initial order; one reported two missed mandate debits; another reported a changed UPI route working later; one reported mandate-approval failure; one reported accidental SIP deletion after pause; one reported an investment pause with trust concerns; one asked about SIP modification choices. These are separate event types and often different platforms. They do not establish investor-level month-12 inactivity. No direct first-time Groww next-payment outcome was found in these reviewed lead sources.

**PM insight (inference):** the most consequential classification error would be to call all of these “SIP dropout.” Stage, intent, actor, control scope and ending differ. The engine is useful for **finding candidate questions** but its broad labels need original-source audit before we choose a segment. The correct next research method is a neutral first-SIP-to-now chronology, followed by a specific event and next-due-date probe if the participant reports one; compare a continuer or informed adjuster on the same broad event type. The current four-lead counts cannot rank reach or month-12 impact.

**Hypotheses to test, not findings:** an initial schedule mental-model gap; an unwanted execution interruption without usable recovery; control consequence misunderstanding; trust change after unresolved status/support. Each requires a direct target-cohort episode with stated consequence and a rival check. A wanted pause, another active SIP, successful later payment or an upstream condition can overturn an apparent continuation problem.

**Immediate decision:** retain all four as **unscored research lanes**, but correct the trust lead's misfit and split payment events by stage. Do **not** select the investor segment, root cause or solution from this pass. The next artifact is a screened, neutral first-hand guide and a small comparison matrix that specifies who would count as a direct Groww case and which outcome is still unknown. If direct participants cannot be obtained, report the limit and test only safe comprehension mechanisms, without claiming validated user demand or NSM effect.
